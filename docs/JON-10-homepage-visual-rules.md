# JON-10 Homepage Visual Rules

Status: updated for JON-41 homepage storyboard prototype
Reference: Lightweight scroll rhythm and premium pacing only
Route target: `/home-storyboard-prototype`

## Core Rule

The rebuilt homepage must be scroll storytelling, not normal website sections.

Use Lightweight as a pacing reference only: pinned stage, evolving central object, sparse text beats, progress feedback, technical scene changes, then release into regular content.

Do not copy Lightweight code, assets, text, product treatment, layout details, model behaviour, branding, or proprietary implementation.

## Non Negotiables

- Use real Joner imagery wherever possible.
- No stock imagery.
- No prices.
- Black, white, and Joner red are the core palette.
- The football development story must feel built from real coaching, real staff, real app, real camps, real HQ, real partners, books, and future tools.
- The prototype should be comparable beside Lightweight for rhythm and polish, but clearly original in story, visuals, and brand language.

## Pacing Rules

- Desktop opens with one pinned storytelling sequence.
- Pin length should feel premium but quick enough to move. For this prototype, use about 70 to 75 viewport height per beat so Philosophy reaches Origin quickly on a normal mouse wheel.
- Do not scroll-jack the full page. Release into normal homepage content after the story sequence.
- Each beat gets a clean hold, then a transition. Do not make visitors scroll several times before the second beat appears.
- Only one main copy beat should be active at a time.
- Leave quiet moments where the image, ball, map, app card, or real clip carries the story.
- First CTA appears in the final pinned beat or immediately after release, not in every scene.
- After release, show real proof quickly: app, training, camps, HQ, staff, and email capture.

## Pinned Object Rules

- The central object slot should remain clean for Lee's final football direction.
- The prototype can use a placeholder football before the real animation is built, but do not spend more time faking the final spin.
- A real football or player-touch moment should remain the visual thread across the sequence.
- Do not use a Nike logo or another brand mark unless Lee has approved rights for that exact usage.
- The object must change meaning as the user scrolls:
  - Philosophy: Technique Above Everything, kick moment, football activated.
  - Origin: 2013, a bag of balls, private sessions, small groups.
  - Growth: online reach, social platforms, numbers building.
  - Energy and Positivity: coaching energy, confidence, technical detail.
  - Global: camps, coaching worldwide, real player energy, and a simple proof graphic.
  - App: session plans, drills, app screens, training in your pocket.
  - Facility: buying The HQ, a real home for the methodology.
  - Action: player, parent, coach pathways.
- Do not make a large abstract circle the main object. Rings are overlays only.
- Final animation direction is separate from this static pass. Keep the object lane ready for the final football.

## Progress Rail Rules

- Desktop gets a slim progress rail on the left side of the pinned stage.
- The rail acts as chapter feedback, not a primary navigation menu.
- Use short labels only:
  - 01 Philosophy
  - 02 Origin
  - 03 Growth
  - 04 Joner Way
  - 05 Global
  - 06 JF App
  - 07 Our HQ
  - 08 Action
- The active chapter should be clear through red progress, opacity, or weight.
- Keep the rail quiet. It should feel like a technical instrument, not a sidebar.
- Mobile should not use the desktop rail. Use small dots, a top progress line, or chapter numbers near the text.

## Scene Transition Rules

- Start black.
- Move through dark technical scenes before any white canvas appears.
- Use white or light grey only for a deliberate technical reveal, such as app, system, or session planning.
- Return to black for the final action beat.
- Red should behave like a precision signal: dots, rails, CTA fills, and active data.
- Avoid decorative gradients. Use light, shadow, and image contrast instead.
- Tactical graphics should be used only when they clarify the coaching story. Remove any graphic that makes the frame look busy or covers the photo.

## Visual System

### Black

- Use black for intensity, origin, premium contrast, and final action.
- Black scenes should still contain real football context through lighting, pitch texture, ball, boot, or player detail.

### White

- Use white sparingly for technical clarity.
- White scenes should feel like session planning, app structure, analysis, or coaching methodology.
- Do not let white sections become generic SaaS cards.

### Red

- Use Joner red as the signature accent.
- Red can mark ball contact points, rail progress, CTA states, and active data.
- Do not flood the page with red backgrounds.

### Real Joner Imagery

- Prioritise real Joner photos and video stills.
- Best image types: ball at foot, Lee coaching, The HQ, small groups, Joners Juniors, JFP, camps, staff, app screens, partners, apparel, books.
- Images must have space for text.
- Text, cards, and graphics must never cover Lee's face, the ball-contact moment, or the main coaching action.
- Images should feel high energy, sharp, and grounded in coaching.
- If a real asset is missing, use a plain placeholder or original generated concept frame marked for replacement. Do not use stock imagery.

### Motion Clips

- Short real Joner clips can appear inside individual beats once the static frames are approved.
- Use clips as texture inside the scene, not as noisy full-screen backgrounds on every beat.
- Best clip moments: ball strike, Lee coaching detail, social growth montage, camp energy, app scrolling, HQ exterior.
- Clips should be muted, compressed WebM or MP4, 1 to 3 seconds, looped, and paused when offscreen.
- Every clip needs a strong poster image so the page still works if video does not load.

### Sparse Copy

- Copy should be short, uppercase, and cinematic.
- One headline per beat.
- Supporting copy should be one short sentence only when needed.
- Avoid paragraph blocks inside the pinned sequence.
- No prices or plan amounts anywhere in the homepage story.

### Tactical Graphics

- Use original tactical graphics only.
- Allowed graphics: pitch grids, cone marks, touch zones, passing lanes, session cards, timing markers, coach cue labels, red contact dots.
- Graphics should look like coaching analysis, not sci-fi decoration.
- Keep line weight thin and precise.
- Avoid random red diagonal lines. Red should feel like data, chapter progress, city pins, or a precise coaching cue.

### Pitch Grids

- Pitch grids can appear as subtle overlays behind real imagery.
- Use them to explain training structure, small groups, movement, and player pathways.
- Grids should fade between beats and never overpower the photo.

### App Cards

- App cards should look like Joner training tools, not generic SaaS tiles.
- Show app value through drill library, session plan, video training, coaches section, and player programs.
- Cards may orbit, stack, or slide around the football anchor during the System beat.
- Do not display app pricing.

### World Map Moments

- The global moment should feel like football development at scale, not a travel agency graphic.
- Avoid city dots and camp labels until the final imagery is stronger.
- Use a simple proof graphic, global language, and real camp energy.
- Keep labels short and readable.

## Mobile Handling

- Mobile must be simpler than desktop.
- Use 3 to 4 beats, not the full desktop sequence if it feels cramped.
- The football or core image must stay visible at all times.
- Avoid long blank pinned areas.
- Text transitions must not overlap or ghost.
- CTA should appear quickly.
- Use mobile progress dots or a thin top progress line.
- If animation fails, the mobile page must still read as a clear vertical storyboard.
- Respect reduced motion with a static storyboard fallback.

## What Not To Copy

- Do not copy Lightweight assets, wheel object, product camera path, layout proportions, text, labels, code, class names, timing values, or brand details.
- Do not copy the exact left rail design. Build a Joner version with football chapter language.
- Do not copy the wheel transformation metaphor.
- Do not make Joner feel like a luxury product catalogue.
- Do not make the sequence abstract for too long. Joner must show real coaching proof early.
- Do not use stock photos, generic football silhouettes, fake app screens, fake testimonials, or price cards.
- Do not let the page become a normal stacked homepage before the pinned story has done its job.

## Prototype Comparison Checklist

Lee should be able to open Joner beside Lightweight and check:

- Does the Joner page pin with similar confidence?
- Does one central story object evolve as the user scrolls?
- Do text beats appear and disappear cleanly?
- Does the progress feedback make the sequence feel intentional?
- Does the scene move from dark intensity to technical clarity and back to action?
- Does mobile feel shorter, clearer, and safer than the desktop sequence?
- Does Joner feel like a global football development company, not a copied product page?
